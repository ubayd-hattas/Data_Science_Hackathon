Cross-city building-age classification
from Landsat spectral time series

Transferring a Madrid-trained model to Amsterdam with a small labelled budget.

<Author A>, <Author B>, <Author C>, <Author D>
Team <name>
Abstract  ·  149 words

Predicting building construction era from 30 m Landsat imagery is hard, and harder across cities: a model trained on Madrid must work in Amsterdam, where building materials, climate and urban form all differ. We compress each pixel’s 40-year, six-band reflectance series into per-pixel temporal statistics and train a class-balanced Random Forest on Madrid. Transfer rests on unsupervised, label-free alignment: class-conditional CORAL reshapes Madrid to Amsterdam one age class at a time, driven by the model’s own pseudo-labels (zero-shot macro-F1 0.36→0.65). Budget-scaled ZCA whitening with a small local classifier, blended with the Stage-1 model and smoothed over map-neighbours, handles the few-shot regime. By 100 labelled Amsterdam pixels per class the transferred model reaches Madrid’s own in-city score (0.72 vs 0.66). Residual error concentrates on pre-1984 classes, which carry no construction event in the record. Simple distribution alignment beat a learned embedding, ordinal loss, self-training and gradient boosting.

§1  Problem & contributions2 / 8

Cross-city domain shift breaks a spectral age model

  • Task: assign each 30 m pixel to one of four construction-era classes; score = macro-F1.
  • Train on Madrid (labelled); adapt to Amsterdam with 5–200 labels per class.
  • Both covariate shift (materials, climate, sensor era) and label shift (class priors differ 1.6× for class 1).
Contributions
  1. Class-conditional CORAL — iterative per-class covariance alignment driven by pseudo-labels; no target labels.
  2. Budget-adaptive few-shot pipeline — whitening strength, model blend and spatial smoothing all scale with n.
  3. Systematic ablation including five reported negative results.
§2  Data & representation3 / 8

108 temporal statistics per pixel

Each pixel carries up to 40 annual six-band observations. We gap-fill, then collapse the series — one row per pixel forces the model onto trends, not per-year snapshots.

  • Overall / early / late / year-on-year mean & SD per band
  • Five spectral indices (NDVI, NDBI, …)
  • Largest single-year jump: magnitude, direction, timing
  • Eight-nearest-neighbour feature averages
Design rationale
  1. Jump-timing features target classes 3–4, whose construction event falls inside the record.
  2. Chosen for construction physics that generalises — not to memorise Madrid.
Madrid: 76,263 pixels  ·  Amsterdam: 25,992 pixels  ·  one QA-valid observation per band-year.

§4  Experimental setup5 / 8

Protocol & model selection

  • Madrid reference: 5×5 repeated stratified CV, macro-F1.
  • Amsterdam: budgets {5, 25, 50, 100, 200} per class, 20 random support draws each.
  • Hyperparameters + feature set from a 500-configuration search scored on one half of Amsterdam, confirmed once on the untouched other half.
Caveats stated up front
  1. Repeated resampling ≠ k-fold CV; ± is dispersion, not a standard error.
  2. ~80% of support pixels have an immediate neighbour in the query set (random per-class sampling; measured).
Fixed seed 42, end-to-end reproducible — notebooks/5-Transfer.ipynb.

§6  Analysis — ablation & failure modes7 / 8

Which components matter, and where the ceiling is

Ablation Δ macro-F1

  • Class-cond CORAL +0.07 zero-shot
  • Budget-scaled whitening +0.15 @ n=5
  • Stage-1 prior blend +0.04 @ n=5
  • Neighbour smoothing +0.01 all n
  • Feature/HP search +0.02

Negative results

  • Triplet embedding − features already separable
  • Ordinal loss 0 smaller errors, not fewer
  • Label-shift EM − priors miscalibrated across gap
  • Self-training − pseudo-label error compounds
  • Gradient boosting − collapses at n=5

Limitations

  • Classes 1 & 2 (pre-1984): no construction event — a data limit, matches the literature.
  • Support/query spatial adjacency (~80%): disclosed; smoothing kept prediction-only.
Every attempt to out-model the data lost to distribution alignment.
§7  Conclusion8 / 8

A calibration problem, not a capability one

  1. A model in a new city is mis-calibrated, not incapable — per-class alignment recovers most of the gap with zero labels.
  2. ~50 labelled pixels per class buy in-city accuracy; the low-data regime needs its own machinery, not a shrunk version of the full one.
  3. On 30 m Landsat, careful distribution use beat every learned-representation alternative we tried.
Originality
  1. Iterative pseudo-label-driven per-class CORAL, and the framing that transfer is a spectrum requiring different mechanics per label budget.
