Guessing building age from space — in a city we’ve never studied

Train on Madrid. Work in Amsterdam. A few hundred checked buildings, no more.

Abstract

A model trained in one city is mis‑tuned in another, not incapable. We reshape the Madrid data to match Amsterdam one age group at a time, using no Amsterdam answers, which lifts the no‑label score from 0.36 to 0.65. A handful of local examples closes the rest: by 50 per group the transferred model matches a home‑city one. Simple alignment beat a neural network, ordinal training, self‑training and gradient boosting.

01 / the problem02 / 07

The same model doesn’t work in a new city

  • We sort each 30‑metre patch of city into one of four age groups.
  • A satellite gives us 40 years of how that patch reflects light.
  • Madrid’s brick and Amsterdam’s concrete reflect it differently — so Madrid’s rules land in the wrong place.
Madrid

warm, dry, older brick

Amsterdam

Same building age — different fingerprint.
02 / our fix03 / 07

Reshape Madrid’s data to look like Amsterdam — before predicting

0.36→0.65
score in Amsterdam with zero local answers
  • We line the two cities up one age group at a time.
  • No Amsterdam answers used — only the model’s own first guesses.
This is the step that does the real work.
03 / a few local examples04 / 07

“Few” needs a different recipe than “many”

  • 5–25 known buildings → mostly trust the Madrid model.
  • 200 known buildings → mostly trust the local one.
  • The mix shifts on its own as the number grows — nothing is hand‑set per case.
The low‑data case isn’t the full pipeline with less data — it’s a deliberately different one.

05 / what we learned06 / 07

The simple fixes won. The clever ones didn’t.

Worked

  • Line the two cities up
  • Blend a local model with the Madrid one
  • Let neighbouring patches vote

Didn’t

  • A neural network
  • Four other well‑known tricks
  • — all beaten by the simpler fixes

Won’t claim

  • The two oldest groups can’t be told apart — the signal isn’t in the data.
  • Some of the score is nearby buildings looking alike. We say so.
Every attempt to out‑think the data lost to using it more carefully.
06 / the point07 / 07

It’s a calibration problem, not a hard one

  1. A model in a new city is mis‑tuned, not incapable.
  2. Fifty local examples buy home‑city accuracy.
  3. Careful use of the data beat every fancier model.
