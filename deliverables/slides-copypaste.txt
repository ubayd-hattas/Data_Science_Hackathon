SLIDE 1   PROBLEM & DATA
============================================================
Predict a building's age group from 40 years of satellite light

- Four construction-era classes per 30 m Landsat pixel; scored on macro-F1
- Train in Madrid, use in Amsterdam with a few local labels
- Same model: 0.66 in Madrid, 0.43 in Amsterdam
- Features chosen for construction physics that transfers, not to fit Madrid

FIGURE: fig_slide1_scatter.png  (Madrid vs Amsterdam feature clouds - apart)
LAYOUT: left text / right image; abstract (below, ~150 words) in a small box across the bottom

ABSTRACT (paste into the small box - rubric requires it on slide 1):
Predicting building construction era from 30 m Landsat imagery is hard, and
harder across cities: a model trained on Madrid must work in Amsterdam, where
building materials, climate and urban form all differ. We compress each pixel's
40-year, six-band reflectance series into per-pixel temporal statistics and train
a class-balanced Random Forest on Madrid. Transfer rests on unsupervised,
label-free alignment: class-conditional CORAL reshapes Madrid to Amsterdam one
age class at a time, driven by the model's own pseudo-labels (zero-shot macro-F1
0.36 -> 0.65). Budget-scaled ZCA whitening with a small local classifier, blended
with the Stage-1 model and smoothed over map-neighbours, lifts the few-shot curve
to 0.66-0.74 at 5-200 labels per class. Auditing our own evaluation, we find
roughly half that few-shot gain reflects support pixels lying next to scored
pixels; the label-free zero-shot result is unaffected. Simple distribution
alignment beat a learned embedding, ordinal loss, self-training and gradient
boosting.

SLIDE 2   WHAT WE DID  -  AND WHY
============================================================
Line the cities up first - the model does the rest
(sub: fix the calibration, not the model - no Amsterdam labels for the big jump)

01 · Line the cities up, one age group at a time   [our idea]
     -> the model's rules are fine; its number-ranges are off
02 · Untangle the features - gently when labels are thin
     -> many of the 108 double-count; the maths needs enough examples to be stable
03 · Blend a small local model with the Madrid one
     -> trust Madrid while labels are few, trust local as they grow
04 · Only the labels we're given ever touch the model
     -> everything else uses unlabelled data only - the rule we never break

SLIDE 3   RESULTS
============================================================
About 50 labels per class reach the in-city ceiling

- Zero labels: 0.36 -> 0.65 (the line-up alone)
- 5 / 25 / 50 / 100 / 200 labels  ->  0.66 / 0.68 / 0.70 / 0.72 / 0.74
- Madrid tested on itself: 0.66; provided-notebook baseline: 0.42 -> 0.67
- Steep to ~50, then flat - the line-up does the work, labels buy the last points

SLIDE 4   WHAT DIDN'T WORK
============================================================
The simple fixes won; the clever ones didn't

- Neural network - nothing to learn, the data was already separable
- Ordinal training - smaller mistakes, not fewer; the score only counts right vs wrong
- Self-training - it learns its own early mistakes
- Gradient boosting - collapses at 5 labels
- Every attempt to out-model the data lost to using it more carefully

SLIDE 5   WHAT WE AUDITED
============================================================
We stress-tested our own few-shot result

- ~80% of support pixels touch a test patch - measured twice
- We re-ran few-shot with the labels held a full map-tile from the scored area
- About half the few-shot gain was proximity, not the labels
- Zero-shot transfer (0.36 -> 0.65) uses no labels, so it is unaffected - that is the solid result
- No rule broken: the organiser's split is random; we just measured what it costs

============================================================
SLIDE 6   SUMMARY
============================================================
Cross-city transfer is a calibration problem, not a hard one

- A new-city model is mis-tuned, not incapable
- About 50 labels per class buy home-city accuracy
- Careful use of the data beat every fancier model
- New here: group-by-group alignment, and that few vs many labels need different pipelines
- Next: spatial-block-disjoint evaluation; a third city
